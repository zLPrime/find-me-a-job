# Skill: Outreach Messaging

## Purpose

Draft short, person-directed messages that sit *outside* a formal
application package — a proactive note to a recruiter or contact about a
role, and a follow-up after an application or an earlier message — grounded
strictly in the candidate profile, for the candidate to review, adjust,
and send themselves.

This is distinct from [application-writing](application-writing.md): that
skill produces the pieces of an application *package* (cover letter,
question answers, submission notes) submitted *through* a posting. This
skill produces the messages a candidate sends *to a person* alongside or
after applying — where the goal is to start a conversation, not to file an
application.

## When to Invoke

- The candidate wants to reach out to a recruiter, hiring manager,
  referral, or networking contact about a specific role or speculatively.
- The candidate wants to follow up on an application already submitted, or
  on an earlier outreach message that went unanswered, after a reasonable
  interval (see [search-strategy](search-strategy.md) for cadence).

## Required Inputs

- The candidate profile (the only source of facts about the candidate).
- The relevant vacancy or employer artifact, if the message is about a
  specific role.
- What is known about the recipient (who they are, and any message or
  profile of theirs being replied to — needed for the language choice
  below).
- For a follow-up: what was previously sent and when.

## Expected Outputs

- A concise draft message, clearly marked as a draft for the candidate to
  review and send themselves — this skill never sends anything, per
  [rules/general.md](../rules/general.md).
- Where useful, a one-line note on why it's framed the way it is (the hook
  it leads with, what reply it invites), so the candidate can judge it.

## Quality Criteria

- **Concise.** A recruiter hook or follow-up is short — a few sentences a
  busy person reads in one glance — not a cover letter. Say who the
  candidate is, the specific hook (the one most relevant, checkable fact
  from the profile that fits this role or this recipient's work), and a
  low-friction ask.
- **Sparks a reply; doesn't beg a favor.** The message opens a
  conversation of mutual interest — it leads with something concretely
  relevant and invites a response — rather than asking the recipient to do
  the candidate a favor ("please consider me," "any help appreciated").
  The ask is easy to say yes to.
- **Specific and human, not generic or AI-written.** Grounded in a real
  fact from the profile and tied to this recipient/role; free of the
  machine-generated tells called out in
  [application-writing](application-writing.md)'s "Human voice" convention
  (hollow superlatives, throat-clearing openers, boilerplate connective
  phrasing). Confidence comes from the fact, not from adjectives.
- **Factual.** Every claim traces to the candidate profile; no invented
  connection to the recipient, mutual contact, or shared history. See
  [rules/factual-accuracy.md](../rules/factual-accuracy.md).
- **Follow-ups stay graceful.** A follow-up references the prior contact
  briefly, adds a reason to re-engage where there is one, and never
  presumes or guilts; a single polite nudge, not a chase.

## Style Conventions

- **Language from the recipient**: like any message to a named person, an
  outreach or follow-up message takes its language from the *recipient*,
  cross-checked against the candidate profile's Languages — per the
  Language selection rule in [rules/outputs.md](../rules/outputs.md) and
  the "Message to a named person" convention in
  [application-writing](application-writing.md). Do not default to English.
- **Sign with name only**: contact details belong on the CV, not repeated
  in a short message.

## Limitations

- Drafts only — the candidate reviews, edits, and sends every message
  themselves; no agent contacts anyone on the candidate's behalf.
- It cannot supply facts about the recipient the candidate hasn't
  provided; a guessed connection is never a substitute — such gaps are
  open questions, not filled in.

## Future Improvements

- Build a small set of outreach archetypes (cold recruiter note, referral
  ask, post-application follow-up, revive-a-dead-thread) with guidance on
  grounding each in the profile without becoming formulaic — mirroring the
  question-archetype idea in [application-writing](application-writing.md).
